ButtonPanel.Children.Insert(0, border) puts the provider indicator at the head of the same StackPanel the targets live in, at the same size, radius and margin as a real button — a 120 px target-shaped thing that cannot be dwelled, sitting inside the target band. It is also not idempotent: Initialize re-adds it, so a settings save with a provider already set appends a second one. The shift this artboard originally claimed does not happen today — SetProviderIndicator has one caller (OverlayManager.cs:108) which runs before the strip is ever centred (App.xaml.cs:467). The hazard is structural, not live: nothing stops a second caller, and the row has no rule that says chrome stays out of it.

The strip reserves a fixed number of slots and centres on that reserved width, not on how many buttons happen to be filled. Chrome — the provider indicator, and anything else that is not dwellable — sits outside the band behind a rule. Turning Calibrate or Quick Actions back on fills slot 6 and slot 7; nothing that was already on screen moves. Empty wells cost 288 px of strip and are worth it: they are also the only honest way to show that the row has room.
And the row itself is long
One row of six: 864 px wide, worst-case travel end to end 720 px.
Three by two: 432 px wide, worst-case travel 322 px — under half, and the whole cluster stays inside the better-calibrated middle of the screen instead of reaching towards both edges.
The cost is real: a docked cluster is 296 px tall against 152 px, so it eats more of the top edge, and the auto-hide summon gesture has to pull back a taller panel. Worth prototyping against the row rather than assuming — this one is a genuine trade, not a fix.
